## Executive Summary

- **New Fed chair Kevin Warsh's first FOMC meeting (2026-07-29/30) added uncertainty rather than resolving it.** The FOMC held rates at 3.50%–3.75% on a split 9-3 vote (three dissenters wanted a hike), and Warsh removed forward guidance — described by ETF Trends as "not a communications change, it's a policy tool." Analysts quoted by Motley Fool/CNBC called his press conference "confusing," "contradictory," and questioned his credibility on inflation. Bond markets did not take it well: per Ritholtz, U.S. borrowing costs jumped to a **19-year high in the biggest one-day move since the "liberation day" tariff announcement**; 30-year yields hit 5.2% (Motley Fool), 10-year at 4.75% / 2-year at 4.28% (ETF Trends, 7/31 snapshot).
- **Inflation is not cooling — it's above target and arguably reaccelerating.** Core PCE at 3.3% (ETF Trends), a FolioBeyond strategist on Yahoo Finance video noting inflation is "higher now than it was a year ago and seems to be reaccelerating," and WSJ reporting Iran-war-driven commodity costs pushing consumer-goods prices higher (beer, paint, fries — Sherwin-Williams, the Samuel Adams brewer). New Section 301 tariffs (10–12.5%) on multiple trading partners took effect in this window (Ritholtz), adding a second inflation vector alongside the war.
- **The Iran/Strait of Hormuz conflict resumed after the July 13 flare-up cooled**, not resolved: WSJ reports the U.S. "hit back at Iran" for an attack on U.S. bases in Jordan (7/30), oil posted "hefty gains" for the month on renewed Iranian attacks on shipping (7/31), and the yen surged on suspected BOJ intervention as Treasury separately warned banks it might intervene in dollar-yen itself — a second, FX-specific volatility channel opening up alongside the oil/rates one.

Separately, Ritholtz flags (via MarketWatch) that **nearly half of small-cap and mid-cap stocks are currently unprofitable** — a specific fragility signal for IWM that the mega-cap-driven index headline numbers don't show.
- **Net read: this batch confirms and extends the July 13 report's HIGH_VOL trajectory, not a mean-reversion back to calm.** A brand-new Fed chair's first meeting added policy uncertainty instead of removing it, the war reignited, a second inflation channel (tariffs) opened, and a new FX-volatility channel (yen intervention risk) appeared — all while equity strength is concentrated in 2-3 mega-cap earnings beats rather than broad participation.
